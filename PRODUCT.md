# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Amazon wholesale sellers doing $500K–$5M in annual revenue, evaluating a new supplier or brand *before* committing to a purchase. They're weighing real money against real uncertainty: is this vendor legitimate, is there any visible link to the brand, and how does the brand actually behave on the marketplace. They are not naive — many have been burned before — but they are not investigators either; they need someone with deep Amazon-wholesale expertise to do the digging and hand back a clear, actionable read.

## Product Purpose

HyprrIQ delivers pre-purchase source-intelligence reports. A client submits a vendor name, brand(s), and optionally an invoice; an AI research pipeline (6 tracks) investigates using public web evidence, the founder reviews and approves, and the client receives a one-page Decision Snapshot with one of four verdicts: Source Clear, Usable With Conditions, Verify Before Purchase, Do Not Rely On This Source.

**The core philosophy, and the thing the whole interface must protect:** the product never confirms vendor authorization — that's unknowable from outside. It surfaces *observable signals* and creates *informed doubt*: a "what to ask your vendor" checklist so the client verifies before buying. Success looks like a client who buys with confidence because they asked the right questions, not because the product told them it's safe.

## Brand Personality

**Authoritative + calm.** Expert, evidence-led, rigorous — but never alarmist, never cold. The product's honest answer is often "here's doubt, go verify," which is a harder sell than a yes/no, so the interface has to carry warmth and trust alongside the rigor, or the honesty reads as anxiety-inducing instead of reassuring. Three words: **rigorous, reassuring, human.**

The interface should feel like a knowledgeable advisor sitting across the table, not a security scanner flashing warnings. Trust and hope — the user should feel more confident about buying after using this, not more afraid.

## Anti-references

- **Generic templated SaaS / "AI slop"** — must not read as a default-template, anonymous AI-generated site. Needs a distinctive identity.
- **Flashy crypto/Web3 style** — no heavy gradients, glow effects, consumer-flashy treatment. Wrong register entirely for a B2B intelligence product.
- **Cold, clinical enterprise software** — capable but joyless, dense gray legacy tooling. Avoid.
- **Alarmist, red-heavy warning UI** — verdicts (especially "Do Not Rely") must read as calm evidence, never as a security-alert klaxon.
- **Depressing dark-mode-as-default** — dark SaaS aesthetics are common right now but risk feeling oppressive or hurting readability for a product whose job is to build buyer confidence. Default to light, warm, optimistic; dark is an option, not the identity.

## Design Principles

1. **Evidence over alarm.** Every visual and copy choice presents findings as calm, sourced fact — never panic, never a siren.
2. **Informed doubt, not false certainty.** No visual treatment (color, iconography, copy) may imply a guarantee the product doesn't make.
3. **Trust before transaction.** Every surface, especially marketing, has to build confidence before asking for payment — this product sells reassurance through rigor, not fear of missing out.
4. **Warmth without diluting rigor.** Friendliness (color, icons, tone) must never undercut the seriousness or credibility of the findings themselves.
5. **Practice what you preach.** UI copy is part of the compliance surface, not just the PDF report — the same banned-language discipline (no "guaranteed," "safe," "authorized" used loosely) applies to every button, tooltip, and empty state.
